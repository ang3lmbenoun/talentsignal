A five-minute product walkthrough

Turn hiring clues into a clearer next step.

TalentSignal helps staffing teams review signs of hiring demand, see where a role may be difficult to fill, and compare the candidate pool before deciding what to do.

Think of it as a weather radar for hiring.
It points your team toward places worth checking. It does not promise what you will find.
Open with the purpose, not the technology. “TalentSignal helps a staffing team make a better-informed decision about which open roles to review and what question to ask next.” Use the weather-radar analogy: a radar gives a useful direction, not a guarantee. This distinction matters because the current app surfaces clues; it does not prove a client will hire or guarantee a placement.

Why a staffing team needs it

Hiring information is scattered. Attention is limited.

Recruiters and sales teams need to know which openings deserve a closer look—and whether their candidate pool is close enough to act.

01

Find the clue

A posting stays open, appears again, or omits pay details. Each clue can change how the team approaches the client.

02

Understand the gap

The role may need skills that the current candidate pool does not show. A match score should lead to a skills conversation, not a blind yes or no.

03

Choose a next step

Confirm the job is still active, clarify requirements, search for more candidates, or prepare a client-facing draft.

Everyday analogy: Instead of searching a whole supermarket aisle with no list, TalentSignal helps staff make a short, explainable shopping list of roles to check first.
Staffing teams already have job boards, applicant systems, spreadsheets, and conversations. The value is making the evidence easier to scan and connect to a next step. Emphasize that the tool supports recruiter judgment; it does not replace the relationship with the client.

From a posting to a human decision

One review path, with the evidence attached.

Notice a clueReview posting age, reposting, salary context, and source quality.
Open the roleSee why it was flagged and whether its role-difficulty evidence is measured, reviewed guidance, or unavailable.
Compare candidatesCheck skill overlap and gaps—only where the app has identifiable role criteria.
Choose an actionVerify, clarify the job description, source more talent, or prepare a draft for a person to review.
Important distinctionDemand evidence asks “does this posting show hiring activity?” Role difficulty asks “might this be harder to fill?” Candidate fit asks “how much skill overlap is visible?” They answer different questions.
Walk across the four steps. The three percentages and evidence labels are not interchangeable. Demand evidence describes posting clues; role difficulty is a separate indicator; candidate fit compares skills to one job. If a role has no identified criteria, fit should be shown as unavailable—not zero. The current app now does that in Targeting.

What should a recruiter take away?

Long-open postings may need an updated sourcing plan. Missing salary context can make it harder to judge whether candidates will align.

  • Confirm the requisition is still active.
  • Ask the client for pay context.
  • Check the source before trusting old dates.
Explain the denominator in ordinary language: 1,124 means the number of postings loaded into this example view. Counts overlap because a posting may be long-open and also lack salary information. The current app’s Lever feed has unusually old dates; its dates are excluded from measured role-scarcity comparisons but still affect age-pattern counts. ✓
A person makes the call before anything goes out.

The product principle is “AI drafts and suggests; a human reviews and releases.” The demo does not send a client message automatically.

In one sentence: TalentSignal helps a staffing team move from “Which postings should we chase?” to “Here is the evidence, here is the gap, and here is the next question to ask.”

Close by returning to the practical value: save time deciding where to look, make the evidence understandable, and turn candidate gaps into better client questions. The Basecamp Trust Before Intelligence principle requires human review and release for anything outbound.
